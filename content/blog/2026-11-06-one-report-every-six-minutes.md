ASD's ACSC has released its seventh [Annual Cyber Threat Report](https://www.cyber.gov.au/publication/annual-cyber-threat-report-2025-26) for 2025-26. The announcement is here: [ASD releases seventh annual Cyber Threat Report 2025-26](https://www.cyber.gov.au/news/asd-releases-seventh-annual-cyber-threat-report-2025-26).

Their own post about it leaned on reporting and collaboration. That is the part I keep coming back to.

## The reporting number

Over 82,000 cybercrime reports went into ReportCyber during the 2025-26 financial year. That is an average of one every 6 minutes, consistent with the year before. ASD also notes that most cybercrime still goes unreported.

Other Year in Review figures from the report:

- over 37,500 calls answered on the Australian Cyber Security Hotline, down 12%
- over 1,500 cyber security incidents responded to, up more than 20%
- more than 4,400 notifications of potential malicious activity, up almost 80%
- average self-reported cost per individual report stayed at $33,000
- average self-reported cost per business report rose 16% to $93,500
- identity fraud remained the top reported cybercrime, down 18%

## Why the report matters

The 2025-26 ACTR covers Australia's cyber threat environment, the impact of rapidly advancing AI, and practical advice for individuals, businesses and critical infrastructure. AI gets a fair amount of airtime this year, as it should. Actors are using it to move faster. Defenders need to as well.

What I do not want to get lost under the AI framing is the basic ask. Report what you see. ASD cannot warn the next organisation about an active campaign if the first one never called it in.

## What I would do with it

- Read the [Year in Review](https://www.cyber.gov.au/publication/annual-cyber-threat-report-2025-26/year-in-review) section if you only have ten minutes. The numbers are clear.
- Grab the factsheet that matches your audience: individuals, businesses, or critical infrastructure. They are linked from the report page.
- If something looks off on a network you look after, report it. [cyber.gov.au](https://www.cyber.gov.au) or 1300 CYBER1 (1300 292 371).

Every report helps build the bigger picture. That line from ASD is not marketing. It is how the next notification gets written.
